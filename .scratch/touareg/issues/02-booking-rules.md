## Question

Bookings are per-Slot (morning / afternoon / night), first-come-first-served, free to cancel. Settle
what remains:

- May a Member hold a run of consecutive Slots across day boundaries, and what is the longest run one
  person may hold so the boat is not blockaded for a fortnight?
- The fairness cap. A per-Member limit on days booked — per Fare year, or per sailing season? Note
  that the Fare year is the calendar year, so "season" would need defining, and this may be the one
  place a season is worth having. When a Member reaches the cap, are they blocked from booking, or may
  the Owner override?
- Two Members want the same Slot. First-come-first-served implies the earlier Booking wins — confirm,
  and say what happens to the loser: does the Slot simply disappear for them, or do they see why?
- May someone book in the past? How far back may a Booking be marked Completed, and what about
  marking a Booking that ended hours ago?
- Are Slots ever unbookable for reasons other than a Booking — a lay-up over winter, a maintenance
  period where the boat is in the yard?

## Answer

**Bookings are per-Slot, first-come-first-served, free to cancel, and every Member books for
themselves.** No Member books on another's behalf, no approval step, no Owner in the path. Settled in
charting and unchanged.

**A Booking may run as long as it likes.** A three-week cruise is one Booking spanning consecutive Slots
across days. Nothing limits its length — the fairness question was about *share*, not trip length, and
capping runs would bite exactly the Members who sail most.

**There is no enforced cap, and no Owner override either.** The fairness question moved out of the
software and into the group: the app shows each Member how many days they have used the boat, and the
Members discuss priority themselves. The Owner cannot cancel another Member's Booking, cannot block a
Member, has no lever at all. When the conversation fails, the only remedy is the conversation.

**What the app does instead of enforcing** is make the number visible, trustworthy, and attributable —
each Member's own figure, and a record of who did what (the Audit log ticket). With eight people the
thing that would break this arrangement is a wrong figure or an unlogged trip, not the absence of a cap.

**A conflict shows whose Slot it is.** The earlier Booking wins and the Slot displays the Member holding
it. A Member may also **register an Interest** in a Slot someone else has, and is told if it is
Cancelled. Interest is not a Booking and confers no priority — there is no queue, and no promotion when
something frees up.

**Cancelling releases the Slots immediately.** A Slot that has **already started cannot be cancelled**,
so a trip that happened cannot be quietly deleted. That is load-bearing now: the fairness conversation
runs on Days Used, and a Member who could erase their own past days could shape the argument.

**A Use can be logged after the fact, for any past date in the current Fare year.** You cannot *reserve*
a Slot that has passed, but you can record having had the boat when no Booking existed, and it counts
exactly as a Completed Booking would. Two edges, both accepted: the window shuts on 1 January, so
December's trips can no longer be logged once the year turns; and a Use cannot be logged inside a
Blocked Period, since a boat in the yard is not being used.

**The Owner can block a date range, with a reason.** Lay-up, a haul-out, time in the yard. Nothing may
be booked inside a Blocked Period, and it is shown as blocked *with its reason* rather than simply
absent — the difference between "the boat's away" and a date that looks broken.

**Carried elsewhere:** the two advisory figures and the fairness view are display questions for the Stats
ticket; Interest, Use and Blocked Period are new terms now in the glossary.
